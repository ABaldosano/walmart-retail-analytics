# Key Insights — Walmart Retail Analytics

Dataset: 9,969 transactions across 100 Walmart branches in 98 US cities, 2019–2023.
Note: 2019 contains only ~1,000 records versus ~2,200+ in other years (partial-year data), so year-over-year comparisons involving 2019 should be read with that caveat.

1. **Two categories drive 80% of revenue.** Fashion Accessories ($489K) and Home & Lifestyle ($489K) together account for roughly 81% of total revenue and 91% of all orders, while the remaining four categories combined contribute under 20%.

2. **High-volume categories have lower ratings.** Fashion Accessories and Home & Lifestyle average a 5.7-5.8 rating despite dominating sales, while low-volume categories like Food & Beverages (7.11) and Health & Beauty (7.0) rate meaningfully higher — suggesting a satisfaction gap in the highest-traffic segments worth investigating.

3. **Credit card is the leading payment method by both volume and revenue.** Credit card transactions account for 4,256 of 9,969 orders (43%) and $488,821 in revenue, narrowly ahead of Ewallet ($457,316) and well ahead of Cash ($263,589).

4. **Revenue has been roughly flat since 2020, after an initial drop.** Full-year revenue moved from $219K (2020) to $233K (2021) to $217K (2022) to $232K (2023) — essentially flat within a ~7% band, with no sustained growth trend across the three most recent complete years.

5. **Revenue is highly concentrated in the top 10 branches.** The top 10 of 100 branches by revenue capture a disproportionate share of total sales, indicating specific branch locations (rather than category mix alone) are a major performance driver worth replicating elsewhere.

6. **Average order value sits around $121, with profit margins near 39%.** Average profit margin across all transactions runs about 39% of order total, giving a healthy baseline for profitability analysis by category or branch.
